Equipment Checklist

What does my child
actually need?
Stage by stage.

Every parent asks the same question when their child starts golf — not "which set scores highest," but "what do I actually need to buy right now, and what can wait?" This checklist matches equipment to JuniorCaddie's five journey stages, so you buy what's needed and skip what isn't.

⛳
JuniorCaddie EditorialMatched to the five-stage Junior Journey · Updated 2026
Before you buy anything
Size by height, never by age — a tall 6-year-old and a small 8-year-old can need the same clubs.
Borrow or buy second-hand first. Buy new once you're confident golf is sticking.
Don't buy a full bag on day one. Most of Stage 1 needs three clubs, not ten.
Outgrown clubs hold real resale value — list them on the JuniorCaddie marketplace rather than letting them sit in the garage.

01
Stage 1 · First Swing
Just starting out
Roughly 3–8 years old · under 125cm · trying golf for the first time
TYPICAL BAG3–5 clubs
FOCUSFun, not performance
SIZINGBy height only
Must have
  • 3–5 clubs (7-iron, wedge, putter, optional wood/hybrid)
  • Lightweight stand or carry bag
  • Glove (one, sized to grow into slightly)
  • Soft compression balls
  • Tees, sized for junior clubs
  • Comfortable trainers or junior golf shoes
  • Suitable clothing — nothing golf-specific required yet
Nice to have
  • A towel for wiping clubs and balls
  • A simple rain jacket
  • Pitch repairer & ball marker, once playing on a course
Don't need yet
  • A full 14-club bag
  • A trolley
  • Spiked golf shoes
  • GPS or rangefinder
  • Custom fitting of any kind
Typical spend
£0 – £150
Browse second-hand → Full club reviews →
JuniorCaddie says
Don't buy a full set for a child who's had three lessons. Ask the club or coach if there's kit to borrow first, then buy second-hand on the marketplace once you know golf is sticking. The Ben Sayers M1i and US Kids UL7 are the standard entry points if you do buy new.
02
Stage 2 · Finding the Game
Playing 9 holes, building interest
Roughly 6–11 years old · 115–140cm · playing regularly, interest confirmed
TYPICAL BAG5–7 clubs
FOCUSTime on course
SIZINGRe-check every season
Must have
  • 5–7 clubs, correctly sized for current height
  • A better-fitting glove
  • Proper golf ball suited to swing speed
  • Waterproof jacket and trousers
  • Golf shoes — spikeless is fine at this stage
  • Pitch repairer and ball marker
Nice to have
  • A trolley, if regularly walking 9+ holes
  • A second glove for wet days
  • Basic alignment or putting practice aid
  • A small towel and brush
Don't need yet
  • A dynamic (motion capture) fitting
  • GPS or rangefinder
  • Multiple wedges or a 3-wood
  • Adult-spec anything
Typical spend
£100 – £250

JuniorCaddie says
This is the stage most families upgrade for the first time — check height against the sizing chart in our club reviews before assuming last year's set still fits. Static fitting off a chart is accurate enough; save paid fitting sessions for Stage 3 onward.
03
Stage 3 · On the Course
Handicap and first competitions
Roughly 9–14 years old · 125–150cm · getting a handicap, entering club and county events
TYPICAL BAG7–10 clubs
FOCUSCourse management
SIZINGStatic fitting recommended
Must have
  • 7–10 clubs, properly fitted for height
  • A trolley
  • Competition-legal GPS or rangefinder (check Local Rule G-5)
  • Spare glove, waterproofs, umbrella
  • Golf shoes suited to typical conditions
  • Towel, pitch repairer, ball marker, spare pencil
Nice to have
  • A proper static fitting session
  • Alignment sticks or a putting gate for practice
  • A dedicated scoring/yardage notebook
Don't need yet
  • A full 14-club competitive bag
  • Adult-length clubs, even if tall for age
  • A launch monitor or swing trainer
Typical spend
£200 – £400
Browse second-hand → Full club reviews →
JuniorCaddie says
First competition nerves are usually about the round, not the kit — but turning up with the wrong gear adds stress that's easy to avoid. Check what's actually allowed under competition rules before buying a rangefinder; see our GPS & rangefinder guidance in the equipment reviews.
04
Stage 4 · Serious Junior
County level and above
Roughly 10–14 years old · 140–170cm · training with intent, competing at county level
TYPICAL BAGUp to 14 clubs
FOCUSKnown yardages
SIZINGDynamic fitting worth it
Must have
  • A properly fitted set, driver through wedges and putter
  • Known carry distance for every club
  • A reliable trolley or lightweight carry setup
  • Tournament-legal rangefinder or GPS
  • Wet-weather kit that's actually been tested
  • Spare of everything — glove, ball marker, pencil
Nice to have
  • A dynamic (motion capture) fitting session
  • Practice aids specific to a known weakness
  • A second wedge for gapping
Don't need yet
  • A full adult-spec bag before physically ready
  • Switching brands mid-season on a hunch
  • Anything bought to match a pro's bag rather than fit
Typical spend
£400 – £800
Browse second-hand → Full club reviews →
JuniorCaddie says
Many Stage 4 players begin transitioning toward adult-spec equipment. That decision should be managed with a coach and fitter — it's about physical development and swing speed, not a birthday. See PING Prodi G and US Kids TS6 in the full reviews for the two main premium routes.
05
Stage 5 · Transition & College Golf
Moving into adult equipment
Roughly 12–16+ years old · 150cm+ · adult or near-adult swing speed
TYPICAL BAG14 clubs, adult-spec
FOCUSCustom fitting
SIZINGBy swing speed, not age
Must have
  • A proper custom fitting — shaft, length, lie, grip size
  • Precise carry yardages for every club, kept current
  • Tournament-standard waterproofs and shoes
  • A bag built for a full round, not a junior carry bag
Nice to have
  • A launch monitor session to confirm shaft fit
  • A second putter to compare feel/weight
  • Travel-specific bag cover for away events
Don't need yet
  • Tour-pro spec equipment bought for the name, not the fit
  • Cutting down an adult club instead of a proper build
Typical spend
£600 – £1,200+
Sell outgrown clubs → Full club reviews →
JuniorCaddie says
The transition moment is when an outgrown junior set has its highest resale value — list it on the marketplace promptly and use the proceeds to offset the new adult set. This is the single most significant equipment investment of the junior journey, so don't rush the fitting to save time.
The bigger picture
Borrow → buy used → buy new → custom fit
That's the order, at every stage — not "buy something, then upgrade, then upgrade again."
Golf clubs are one of the few pieces of kit where the second-hand market is genuinely excellent, because children outgrow clubs long before the clubs wear out. A set bought used at Stage 2 and sold on at Stage 3 can cost almost nothing in real terms.
Coaching programmes often lend kit
Many club junior programmes have clubs juniors can borrow before a family buys anything. Ask before you shop.
True cost, not RRP
A £250 set bought used for £130 and resold a year later for £90 has cost the family £40 for a season of golf — factor resale in before you buy new.
