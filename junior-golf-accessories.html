Which hand does the glove go on?
A golf glove is worn on the lead hand — the hand closest to the target at address. For a right-handed golfer, that's the left hand; for a left-handed golfer, it's the right hand. This is the opposite of what some parents expect, so double-check before buying — a glove ordered for the wrong hand can't easily be swapped once it's been tried on.

Golf Balls for Junior Golfers
There's no such thing as a "junior" golf ball — juniors play standard balls, matched to swing speed rather than age. Lower compression suits slower, developing swings; here's how the five most relevant options compare.
Compression, in plain English
Compression measures how much a ball deforms on impact. A slower swing speed can't compress a firm ball — the energy is wasted and distance suffers. A junior with a developing swing almost always benefits from a lower-compression ball (under ~60), which springs off the face more easily even at modest clubhead speed. As swing speed increases through Stage 4–5, a firmer, more premium ball starts to make more sense.

At roughly 35 compression, the Duo Soft+ is about as low as compression gets on the UK market — which makes it the correct starting ball for the youngest, slowest-swinging juniors on this list. Even a modest Stage 1 swing speed compresses this ball properly, converting effort into distance rather than a dead, clicky feeling off the face.

It also comes in high-visibility colour options, which is a genuinely practical detail for young beginners who lose balls in the rough more often than they'd like to admit.

"If your child's drives feel like they're going nowhere no matter how hard they swing, the ball is very often the problem, not the swing."

The Supersoft has been a mainstream favourite for years, and it earns that reputation honestly. At a low compression that still sits a touch firmer than the Wilson Duo Soft, it launches easily and forgives off-centre contact — exactly the shot pattern a developing Stage 2–4 junior produces most often — while lasting noticeably longer in play than the very softest balls on this list.

It's the sensible middle-ground recommendation: soft enough to help a moderate swing speed, durable enough that a junior isn't churning through a sleeve every round.

The safe, sensible default for most juniors from Stage 2 onward. Soft, forgiving, holds up well, and widely available. If in doubt, buy this one.

Titleist's TruFeel is the softest ball the brand makes, but it's worth being honest that it's firmer than every other ball on this list — a deliberate design choice that makes it the sensible bridge for an older Stage 4–5 junior heading towards a proper premium ball like the Pro V1 as their swing speed and competitive level increase.

You're paying a premium for the Titleist name here relative to the performance-for-price of the Supersoft or Duo Soft, and for most juniors still at Stage 1–3 that premium isn't worth it yet. But for a competitive older junior who wants to start getting used to how a proper tour-style ball behaves, it's the right introduction.
